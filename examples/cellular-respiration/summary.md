# Cellular respiration — written summary (control group for the recall test)

Cellular respiration converts glucose into ATP in three stages. **Glycolysis** happens in the cytoplasm without
oxygen: one glucose (6C) is split into two pyruvate (3C), using 2 ATP and making 4, for a net gain of 2 ATP, plus
2 NADH. In the **mitochondrial matrix**, each pyruvate becomes acetyl-CoA, which joins oxaloacetate to form citrate in
the **Krebs cycle**. Each turn releases 2 CO₂ and makes 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂ and 1 ATP; the cycle turns twice per glucose.
On the **inner mitochondrial membrane**, the **electron transport chain** takes electrons from NADH and FADH₂ and uses
their energy to pump H⁺ ions into the space between the membranes. H⁺ flows back through **ATP synthase**, which
makes about 26–28 ATP. **Oxygen** is the final electron acceptor and forms water. In total, aerobic respiration
makes about 30–32 ATP per glucose. Without oxygen, cells use **fermentation**: muscles make lactate and yeast make
ethanol and CO₂. Fermentation makes no extra ATP; it regenerates NAD⁺ so glycolysis can continue.
